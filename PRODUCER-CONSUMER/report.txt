Producer-Consumer Problem — CS471 Report
=========================================

--- Shared Variables & Synchronization Primitives ---

1. SharedBuffer::buf_  (vector<SalesRecord>)
   Shared between ALL producer and consumer threads.
   Protected by: SharedBuffer::mutex_ (std::mutex)
   File: src/library/buffer.hpp, buffer.cpp

2. SharedBuffer::empty_slots_  (std::counting_semaphore, init = b)
   Producers acquire() before writing — blocks when buffer is full.
   Consumers release() after reading — signals a slot is free.
   File: src/library/buffer.hpp, buffer.cpp

3. SharedBuffer::filled_slots_  (std::counting_semaphore, init = 0)
   Consumers acquire() before reading — blocks when buffer is empty.
   Producers release() after writing — signals a record is ready.
   File: src/library/buffer.hpp, buffer.cpp

4. SharedBuffer::mutex_  (std::mutex)
   Guards the critical section (head_, tail_, buf_) during each
   individual produce() and consume() call.
   File: src/library/buffer.hpp, buffer.cpp

5. total_produced_  (std::atomic<int>, shared across all producers)
   Each producer atomically claims the next slot via fetch_add().
   When the claimed slot >= 1000 the producer exits.
   No mutex needed — std::atomic guarantees sequential consistency.
   File: src/library/producer.hpp, producer.cpp

6. GlobalStats::mutex_  (std::mutex)
   Held by each consumer when it finishes: serialises the local-stats
   print AND the merge into global totals so output never interleaves
   and the aggregate is updated atomically.
   File: src/library/stats.hpp

Records use pseudo-random number generators (std::mt19937) seeded
independently per producer thread via std::random_device.
